# SmartCounter competitor and reference benchmark

**Observed:** 2026-08-13 (Asia/Jakarta)  
**Scope:** first-party public websites in people counting, retail/mall footfall, visitor analytics, and physical-space analytics. This note benchmarks website and product communication quality; it is not a claim that SmartCounter has any capability described below.  
**Evidence rule:** observations are tied to the linked first-party page. Vendor claims are reported as vendor claims, not independently validated. UNKNOWN means the public crawl did not provide enough evidence. Mobile observations are UNKNOWN unless a page exposed direct mobile evidence; no responsive behavior is inferred from desktop HTML.

## Executive benchmark verdict

Seven first-party sites were inspected across home, product/platform, industry/solution, proof/resource, and contact/demo surfaces. The strongest communication pattern is not “more features”; it is a short path from a named physical-space problem to a metric, an operational decision, and credible proof.

## 1. RetailNext — top reference

### What the site communicates

- **Hero/value proposition:** “Retail Intelligence, One Question Away” anchors the category in retail intelligence, then explains that Pulse AI sits on a large shopping-trip dataset and answers plain-language questions about a store floor. The hero pairs a strong category noun with a concrete interaction promise and one primary CTA, “Book a Discovery Call” ([home](https://retailnext.net/)).
- **Information architecture:** the global nav separates Platform (Traffic Analytics, Insights, Asset Protection, Aurora sensor), Solutions by sector and use case, Benchmarks/Pricing/Resources, Partners, About, Support, and Contact. This gives an evaluator multiple entry paths without hiding the core products ([home](https://retailnext.net/)).
- **Product explanation:** Traffic Analytics starts from the operational problem (“staffing, scheduling, store hours” based on guesswork), then names accuracy, conversion, labor optimization, benchmarking, and forecasting; Insights explains pilot/control-store testing before rollout. This is a coherent “measure → decide → validate → scale” story rather than a feature grid ([Traffic Analytics](https://retailnext.net/platform/traffic-analytics), [Insights](https://retailnext.net/platform/insights)).
- **Retail/mall segmentation:** Shopping Malls has a dedicated narrative around leasing, campaign attribution, tenant analytics, live occupancy, and zone value. The page speaks to property outcomes rather than merely repeating store metrics ([Shopping Malls](https://retailnext.net/solutions/shopping-malls)).
- **Visual assets and motion:** the crawl exposes product/image slots, Aurora sensor imagery, customer-logo marks, quote blocks, and a resource library with case studies, videos, and events. Exact animation timing and visual polish are UNKNOWN without a viewport capture; do not infer them from HTML asset labels ([home](https://retailnext.net/), [Resources](https://retailnext.net/resources)).
- **Proof:** the customer-story page names 600+ retailers across 100+ countries, shows customer logos, and presents outcomes such as shopper-yield increase, additional sales, conversion improvement, and forecasting accuracy; individual stories include challenge, solution, and result context ([Customer Stories](https://retailnext.net/platform/customer-stories)).
- **CTA/demo flow:** “Book a Discovery Call” repeats at hero, product, solution, and footer positions. The CTA is low-friction for enterprise evaluation but the public pages do not expose a self-serve interactive demo; form fields and response SLA are UNKNOWN from the inspected pages ([home](https://retailnext.net/), [Traffic Analytics](https://retailnext.net/platform/traffic-analytics), [Shopping Malls](https://retailnext.net/solutions/shopping-malls), [Contact](https://retailnext.net/about/contact-us)).
- **Footer/company:** footer groups Platform, Solutions, Resources, About Us, Connect, newsletter, and the same discovery CTA. The About page adds founding year, retailer focus, global footprint, offices, and recognition, which gives procurement context beyond product copy ([Who We Are](https://retailnext.net/about/who-we-are), [Resources](https://retailnext.net/resources)).
- **Mobile evidence:** UNKNOWN; no mobile viewport capture was available in this research run.

### What SmartCounter should learn / not copy / adapt

| Learn | Do not copy | Principle worth adapting |
|---|---|---|
| Route each audience to a distinct operational job: store operations, merchandising, store design, marketing, or mall leasing ([home](https://retailnext.net/), [Shopping Malls](https://retailnext.net/solutions/shopping-malls)). | Do not reproduce the breadth of enterprise modules or imply Pulse AI, asset protection, forecasting, or benchmarks unless SmartCounter can evidence them. | Build a small decision tree: **Retail**, **Mall**, and **Platform**; each page names the decision improved, metric used, and proof available. |
| Use “what changes after measurement?” as the section logic; RetailNext repeatedly links counts to staffing, conversion, leasing, and campaign decisions ([Traffic Analytics](https://retailnext.net/platform/traffic-analytics), [Insights](https://retailnext.net/platform/insights)). | Do not use “AI” as the hero if the buyer still cannot tell what is measured. | Put the plain category and outcome before technology. |
| Treat case studies as evidence with challenge → deployment → result, not as a logo wall ([Customer Stories](https://retailnext.net/platform/customer-stories)). | Do not publish percentage or dollar outcomes without scope, denominator, timeframe, and customer permission. | Use one verifiable proof card per primary use case, with a link to the full story. |

## 2. Ariadne — top reference

### What the site communicates

- **Hero/value proposition:** “Understand every visit. Respect every visitor.” immediately combines visitor analytics with a privacy stance. The subhead names Hybrid Fusion, Time-of-Flight, no cameras, no PII, GDPR-native, and EU AI Act readiness ([home](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/)).
- **Information architecture:** the site separates Platform, Solutions, Industries, Resources, Company, and Contact. The home page moves from platform/technology/product/connectivity to solutions, industries, testimonials, hardware, resources, FAQ, and a demo, which mirrors evaluator questions in a compact path ([home](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/)).
- **Product explanation:** the People Counting page defines footfall, occupancy, dwell time, and flow in plain language, then shows how signal sensing and ToF form one dataset. It also describes calibration, validation, audits, privacy boundaries, and deployment options ([People Counting](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/solutions/people-counting/)).
- **Retail/mall relevance:** home explicitly lists Retail Stores and Shopping Centres as industries; the customer directory filters Retail Stores and Shopping Malls; the Giorgio Armani story provides a named retail example ([home](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/), [Customers](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/customers/), [Giorgio Armani story](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/customers/giorgio-armani/)).
- **Visual assets and motion:** trust badges, dashboard images, a “two minutes” product-in-motion video, sensor images, metric diagrams, and customer logos/quotes are visible in the HTML. Exact motion choreography and visual quality are UNKNOWN without a viewport capture ([home](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/), [People Counting](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/solutions/people-counting/)).
- **Proof:** the public pages state deployment counts and show named stories (Giorgio Armani, Praktiker, Watsons, airports, and others); the claims remain vendor-reported and should not be transplanted into SmartCounter messaging without equivalent evidence ([home](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/), [Customers](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/customers/), [Giorgio Armani story](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/customers/giorgio-armani/)).
- **CTA/demo flow:** the call to action is specific: “Two questions, twenty minutes, a real walkthrough of your venue’s footfall.” The page lists what the prospect will see (venue map, live sensor outputs, and fit/no-fit discussion), lowering ambiguity in the conversion step ([home](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/), [FAQ](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/faq/)).
- **Footer/company:** footer includes platform, solutions, industries, resources, company, contact, newsletter, and regional offices including Singapore, which supports its regional credibility ([home](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/)).
- **Mobile evidence:** UNKNOWN; no mobile viewport capture was available in this research run.

### What SmartCounter should learn / not copy / adapt

| Learn | Do not copy | Principle worth adapting |
|---|---|---|
| State privacy architecture as a product fact, then explain what is and is not collected ([home](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/), [People Counting](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/solutions/people-counting/)). | Do not claim “no PII,” GDPR, EU AI Act readiness, or certifications without SmartCounter’s legal/technical evidence. | Add a short “Data boundary” panel: source, processing location, retained output, and what the system does not collect. |
| Define each metric before showing a dashboard: footfall, occupancy, dwell, and flow are tied to decisions ([People Counting](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/solutions/people-counting/)). | Do not use “flow,” “journey,” or “conversion” as unlabeled decorative nouns. | Give each SmartCounter metric a one-sentence definition and one buyer decision. |
| Make the demo promise concrete and time-bounded ([FAQ](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/faq/)). | Do not promise a “live walkthrough” if the sales team cannot provide a venue-map or real product demonstration. | Tell the visitor what happens after they click: information needed, meeting length, and output. |

The product page exposes product comparison and specs. Exact animation behavior is UNKNOWN without viewport capture ([home](https://v-count.com/), [Products](https://v-count.com/products/)).
- **Proof:** the client page names global brands and reports customer-specific outcomes; the page mixes downloadable case studies and video stories, which is useful proof architecture but still vendor-reported ([Clients](https://v-count.com/clients/)).
- **CTA/demo flow:** “Get a Demo,” “Book a Free Demo,” “Get a Quote,” and “See the products live” are repeated at key decision points. Public form fields/qualification sequence are UNKNOWN from the inspected pages ([home](https://v-count.com/), [Products](https://v-count.com/products/)).
- **Footer/company:** the footer has product, contact, news, company, social links, and support; the site also offers multiple languages, which may help regional buyers but adds navigation weight ([home](https://v-count.com/)).

### What SmartCounter should learn / not copy / adapt

| Learn | Do not copy | Principle worth adapting |
|---|---|---|

| Make case studies browsable by recognizable customer and use case ([Clients](https://v-count.com/clients/)). | Do not use logo density to substitute for a named, permissioned customer result. | Prefer two deep case studies over an unverified wall of logos. |

## Cross-site patterns SmartCounter should adopt

### 1. Hero rule: category first, outcome second, proof third

The strongest pages tell visitors what the product is before adding AI language: RetailNext starts with retail intelligence, Ariadne with privacy-first people counting, Density with plug-and-play people counting, and V-Count with people counting/retail analytics ([RetailNext](https://retailnext.net/), [Ariadne](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/), [Density](https://density.io/), [V-Count](https://v-count.com/)). SmartCounter should make “people counting / visitor analytics for retail and malls” legible in the first viewport; a clever abstract headline can be a supporting line, not the only explanation.

### 2. IA rule: separate platform, use case, proof, and company

RetailNext, Ariadne, V-Count, and Density all distinguish some combination of product/platform, industry/use case, resources/case studies, and company/trust pages ([RetailNext home](https://retailnext.net/), [Ariadne home](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/), [V-Count home](https://v-count.com/), [Density home](https://density.io/)). SmartCounter needs fewer routes, not a flat single-page feature dump: a practical minimum is **Retail**, **Mall**, **Platform**, **Proof**, **Technology/Privacy**, **About**, and **Book Demo**.

### 3. Product-story rule: map metric → decision → evidence

The best product pages explicitly connect metrics to actions: traffic to conversion/staffing, journeys to layout, zones to leasing, occupancy to safety, or dwell to engagement ([RetailNext Traffic Analytics](https://retailnext.net/platform/traffic-analytics), [RetailNext Shopping Malls](https://retailnext.net/solutions/shopping-malls), [Ariadne People Counting](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/solutions/people-counting/), [V-Count Retail Store Analytics](https://v-count.com/retail-store-analytics/)). SmartCounter should not expose “heatmap,” “journey,” or “demographics” without a short definition and the business question it answers.

### 4. Proof rule: named, scoped, and inspectable beats impressive numbers

RetailNext, V-Count, Ariadne, FootfallCam, and Trakomatic use named customers, quotes, case studies, or deployment pages ([RetailNext Customer Stories](https://retailnext.net/platform/customer-stories), [V-Count Clients](https://v-count.com/clients/), [Ariadne Customers](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/customers/), [FootfallCam Case Studies](https://www.footfallcam.com/Resources/CaseStudy), [Trakomatic About](https://www.trakomatic.com/about)). SmartCounter should publish only permissioned proof with timeframe, location/use case, metric definition, and methodology; an unverified logo or uplift claim is a trust liability.

### 5. Privacy rule: explain the data boundary, not just the adjective

FootfallCam, V-Count, Ariadne, Density, and RetailNext each put privacy/anonymity claims in product or trust copy ([FootfallCam](https://www.footfallcam.com/), [V-Count](https://v-count.com/), [Ariadne](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/), [Density](https://density.io/), [RetailNext Traffic Analytics](https://retailnext.net/platform/traffic-analytics)). SmartCounter should state input source, edge/cloud processing, retained output, access controls, and deletion/retention policy only where documented. “Privacy-first” without a boundary invites buyer and legal questions.

### 6. CTA rule: promise the next step

The strongest demo CTAs tell the visitor what happens after the click: RetailNext says discovery call, Ariadne says 20-minute venue-map walkthrough, Density describes live demo/ROI, and V-Count says live product demo ([RetailNext](https://retailnext.net/), [Ariadne FAQ](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/faq/), [Density demo](https://demo.density.io/), [V-Count products](https://v-count.com/products/)). SmartCounter should use one primary CTA and a short “what you get” sentence, not a generic “Contact us” button repeated without context.

### 7. Visual rule: real product UI first; explain the physical-to-data bridge

Across the strongest sites, dashboards, sensors, installation photos, flow maps, metric illustrations, and case-study media are used as evidence or explanation—not just decoration ([Density Atlas](https://density.io/atlas), [Ariadne People Counting](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/solutions/people-counting/), [V-Count](https://v-count.com/), [FootfallCam Shopping Malls](https://www.footfallcam.com/Industries/ShoppingMalls), [Xovis Retail](https://www.xovis.com/solutions/retail)). SmartCounter should prioritize actual dashboard captures, an authentic deployment photo, and one camera/sensor → event → analytics diagram. Generic people silhouettes or stock mall photography should not carry the product explanation.

### 8. Motion rule: animate only to explain or confirm

The public crawls expose videos, product-in-motion sections, interactive product tabs, and campaign experiences on several sites, but exact interaction behavior was not measurable without a viewport capture ([Ariadne](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/), [V-Count](https://v-count.com/), [FootfallCam](https://www.footfallcam.com/), [Xovis PF retail](https://discover.xovis.com/pf-retail)). Applying Emil-style restraint: SmartCounter motion should explain a flow/zone transition, confirm a filter/state change, or reveal a product relationship; decorative perpetual motion is not evidence of product quality. Keep functional transitions fast and interruptible; test prefers-reduced-motion.

## What SmartCounter should not copy

- **Unverified metrics:** “99% accuracy,” “30,000 businesses,” ROI multipliers, and conversion uplifts are vendor claims tied to their instrumentation and methodology; they are not portable proof ([FootfallCam](https://www.footfallcam.com/), [V-Count](https://v-count.com/), [Ariadne](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/), [RetailNext Traffic Analytics](https://retailnext.net/platform/traffic-analytics)).
- **Feature sprawl:** FootfallCam and V-Count show how a large catalog can become noisy even when each item is useful ([FootfallCam](https://www.footfallcam.com/), [V-Count](https://v-count.com/)).
- **Technology-first hero copy:** sensor names, AI labels, and acronyms are not a substitute for “what is measured and what decision changes” ([Xovis Technology](https://www.xovis.com/technology), [Trakomatic Products](https://www.trakomatic.com/products)).
- **Privacy overclaiming:** “no PII,” “GDPR-compliant,” “EU AI Act ready,” or “ethical AI” require architecture, legal review, and documentation; SmartCounter should not inherit a competitor's wording ([Ariadne](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/), [V-Count](https://v-count.com/), [Density](https://density.io/)).
- **Logo walls without context:** logos help only when tied to a permissioned story, deployment scope, and outcome ([RetailNext Customer Stories](https://retailnext.net/platform/customer-stories), [V-Count Clients](https://v-count.com/clients/)).
- **Cloned visual identity:** the Xovis comic, Density's product tone, or another site's dashboard framing can inspire principles but must not be copied as artwork, copy, or branded interaction ([Xovis PF retail](https://discover.xovis.com/pf-retail), [Density](https://density.io/)).

## Grill-me / red-team checks before SmartCounter adopts a benchmark idea

1. **Does this make the first viewport clearer?** If a proposed visual or AI statement does not answer what SmartCounter measures and for whom, cut it.
2. **Can sales/support deliver the promise?** A “live demo,” “existing CCTV reuse,” “real-time” claim, or response SLA should be backed by an owner, test, and current artifact.
3. **What is the denominator?** Any conversion, accuracy, dwell, or uplift claim needs a defined numerator, denominator, time window, site scope, and methodology.
4. **Is this a buyer decision or a feature?** If the section does not help a retail operator, mall operator, leasing/marketing team, or technical evaluator decide, it belongs in a secondary page—or not at all.
5. **Would privacy/legal approve the exact sentence?** Replace generic privacy adjectives with documented data boundaries, or mark the claim UNKNOWN until confirmed.
6. **Does the page work without motion or a high-end dashboard image?** Core meaning must survive reduced motion, slow loading, and a narrow viewport.
7. **Is a regional proof actually regional?** A translated page is not local readiness; show Indonesia/SEA deployment, support, timezone, language, and legal/contact evidence only when verified.

## Recommended benchmark takeaways for SmartCounter

1. **Adopt RetailNext's information sequence:** category → operational problem → platform/product → role-based outcomes → customer proof → CTA ([RetailNext home](https://retailnext.net/), [Traffic Analytics](https://retailnext.net/platform/traffic-analytics), [Customer Stories](https://retailnext.net/platform/customer-stories)).
2. **Adopt Ariadne's metric and privacy discipline:** define footfall/occupancy/dwell/flow and state the data boundary before making trust claims ([Ariadne People Counting](https://www.peoplecounting.ai/solutions/people-counting/)).
3. **Adopt V-Count's physical journey framing:** approach/entry/zone/queue is easier to understand than a list of analytics modules ([V-Count](https://v-count.com/)).
4. **Borrow Density's product polish selectively:** real UI, hardware, installation, and a concrete demo promise; do not import its workplace positioning or pricing assumptions ([Density home](https://density.io/), [Density Atlas](https://density.io/atlas), [Density demo](https://demo.density.io/)).
5. **Use Trakomatic's regional proof pattern:** named SEA locations, local contact paths, and existing-infrastructure questions; improve the copy discipline and legal specificity ([Trakomatic Case Studies](https://www.trakomatic.com/case-studies), [Trakomatic Contact](https://www.trakomatic.com/contact-us)).

## Final benchmark conclusion

The defensible winning direction is **product-led, evidence-led, and physically grounded**: explain a retail or mall decision, show the metric and its definition, connect the physical space to the dashboard, then prove the deployment and offer a concrete demo. SmartCounter does not need the largest sitemap or the loudest AI claim; it needs the shortest trustworthy path from **“What is this?”** to **“This answers my operating question, and I know what happens next.”**

**Open verification items for the next PRD phase:** SmartCounter-specific customer permissions, current product screenshots, supported sensors/cameras, deployment/install process, privacy/retention/security language, integration matrix, local case-study scope, conversion form fields/CRM path, page-speed baseline, and mobile viewport testing are all outside this competitor-only note and remain UNKNOWN here.
